Current research · 2026

The Canary in the Dragnet

How migrants became the testing ground for AI surveillance — and what rights-based protection would require.

Submitted to the Center for Humane Technology's Emerging Voices in AI & Society Fellowship. The flagship research anchors in the Asia–Gulf overseas-worker corridor and treats US and EU actors as the supply side and the accountability target — the vendors and platforms exporting the tooling.

Three questions: which surveillance capabilities are deployed on migrant populations first, and through what commercial and state channels; how normalisation on non-citizens lowers the barrier to deployment on citizens; and what rights-based protection would require in law, in technical design, and in procurement to interrupt that diffusion.

It builds directly on the Passport Confiscation Matrix and the 2018 Paris thesis below — the same corridor, examined with a new instrument.

A multiple-embedded case study of Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates and Kuwait, testing anti- and pro-neoliberal explanations for why Gulf states sustain the kafala system despite their ILO commitments — and what human-rights clauses in bilateral agreements would have to look like to bind. Policy analysis & governance

Columbia SIPA and beyond

  • Proposed Enhancements to the Monitoring Framework for FFD4 — capstone for UN DESA My Columbia SIPA capstone, delivered to the UN Department of Economic and Social Affairs ahead of the Fourth International Conference on Financing for Development. The team built a monitoring framework for FFD4 commitments across every chapter of the agenda — domestic public resources, private business and finance, development cooperation, trade, debt — establishing a baseline against the Addis Ababa Action Agenda, identifying legacy measurement gaps, and proposing SMART and proxy indicators with the institutional stakeholders and data sources mapped against each one. Built through consultation with UN DESA's Financing for Sustainable Development Office and expert interviews, including Professor José Antonio Ocampo. Academic advisor: Dr. Fumiko Sasaki. Team project. On request2025 · UN deliverable
  • Permanent Supportive Housing in NYC: A Cost-Benefit Analysis of Breaking Ground A full cost-benefit analysis of permanent supportive housing as an alternative to shelter provision, set against the erosion of the Callahan right-to-shelter consent decree and a shelter population that grew 19% between 2022 and 2024. On request2025 · CBA
  • The War on Drugs and Human Rights Violations: The Philippines, 2016–2022 An examination of the Duterte-era anti-narcotics campaign, the resulting police-killing figures and the ICC's investigation — written by someone who was serving in the Philippine legislature for the back half of the period under examination. On request2024 · Human rights
  • Comparing Paramilitary Groups and Practices of Policing: The United States and the Philippines A comparative study of criminal-justice structures, police use of force and the institutions meant to regulate them across two very different constitutional systems. On request2024 · Comparative
  • Summary and Critique of the New York City AI Policy An analysis of the city's five thematic areas — data infrastructure, application, policy and governance, cross-sector partnerships, and workforce — and of the digital-rights framework underneath them, including where privacy, accountability, fairness and transparency come into direct tension with one another. On request2024 · AI policy
