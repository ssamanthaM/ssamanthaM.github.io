Applied work

Five systems, one question.

What happens when an automated system makes a consequential decision about someone who has no standing to contest it — and who decides what the system is not allowed to do? Each project below is one answer, built and running.

Project 01 — 2024 to present

Handa

Model guardrails & escalation for AI legal aid

A responsible-AI legal-aid platform for overseas Filipino workers, built on the Claude API. The users are people facing wage theft, passport confiscation and contract substitution, usually in a jurisdiction that offers them no remedy, usually not in their first language, usually with no lawyer and no leverage.

That user profile sets the entire safety posture. A confidently wrong answer here means somebody acts on bad legal guidance in a country where the mistake cannot be undone. So the design question became the boundary itself: which answers the model may give, and what happens at the edge of that permission.

The rule set

Answer, escalate, or refuse

Every query resolves into one of three outcomes. The model may answer directly where guidance is general and low-risk. It must route to a human reviewer where the stakes are individual and legal. It must refuse outright where an answer would constitute advice it has no basis to give.

The gate

Evaluate before you show

Pre-response evaluation for accuracy, bias and hallucination, with confidence-threshold gating. The harness exists to answer one question before anything reaches a user: is this safe to show a worker with no lawyer?

The assumption

The model will be confidently wrong

The escalation path is designed on that premise. Red-teaming targets the specific failure modes that matter here — hallucinated legal guidance, biased outputs, unsafe autonomous action — and each finding becomes an intervention checkpoint.

How it is built

  • Front end — React Native, so it reaches workers on the phone they already have.
  • Reasoning layer — the Claude API, with prompts and evaluation criteria written in house.
  • Services — Node.js.
  • Data — Supabase.
  • Review loop — an evaluation harness that tests guardrail decisions against real queries.

Who it is built with

A six-tier partner ecosystem spanning government, multilateral, NGO, technology and legal organisations — including OWWA, the International Labour Organization, Lawyers Without Borders and Migrant Forum in Asia — mapped onto co-marketing and co-delivery pathways.

Addressable population: more than two million overseas Filipino workers. Every function — product strategy, build, partner development, go-to-market — run by one person.

Project 02 — ongoing

WPS Maritime Tracker

An incident-to-award evidentiary tracker for the West Philippine Sea

As a legislator I represented the Philippines in ASEAN fora negotiating West Philippine Sea maritime rules. This is the same dispute, approached with a different instrument: every incident — water cannon, ramming, blocking, laser, boarding and seizure, militia swarming, environmental harm — recorded as a structured case record that maps the act to specific UNCLOS articles, COLREGs rules, and holdings of the 2016 South China Sea Arbitral Award (PCA Case No. 2013-19).

The fields are informed by the Berkeley Protocol on Digital Open Source Investigations: provenance, chain of custody, source tiering, and a methodology that can be disclosed to an opposing party. It is built for the people who would actually have to file: the Solicitor General's office, Coast Guard legal, AMTI and Stratbase, and international counsel.

Custody

Tamper-evident by construction

The custody log is an append-only SHA-256 hash chain: each entry commits to its own row and the previous entry's hash, with fields joined on a separator that cannot occur in any field — so an attacker cannot shuffle bytes across a field boundary without breaking the chain. Updates and deletes are blocked at the database by triggers, and digests are anchored via OpenTimestamps. Any evidence item with an empty custody log is flagged not filing-ready in the interface.

Disclosure

Publication is a whitelist

A record is public only if it is marked stable and its feature sits in an active corpus phase. Only views named v_pub_* may ever be wired to a route; internal, ungated views keep plain names and are structurally barred from public exposure. Expanding scope is a single data change — which means the decision about what is disclosed stays in one reviewable place.

Epistemics

Honest about what it doesn't know

Award paragraph numbers are marked unverified against the primary text, and every citation in the UI says so. AIS is self-reported, and militia vessels run dark — so absence of a track is not absence of a ship, and the app says that on the layer itself. Proximity is correlation, not proof. Confidence reflects reporting quality, not independent verification.

Where the AI sits — and where it is not allowed to

A refresh pipeline pulls open-source reporting and asks Claude to structure genuine incidents into the schema. Every record it produces enters as a draft — written with confidence reported and verification status unverified — and cannot reach the published view without human adjudication.

That is the same rule as Handa, in a domain where the stakes are a filing: the model proposes, a human disposes, and the boundary between the two is enforced by the data model itself.

Corroboration from independent data. Each dossier auto-queries Global Fishing Watch for encounter, loitering and AIS-gap events within ±7 days and 75 km of the incident, sorted by distance and day-offset and plotted on the locator map.

The 17 June 2024 Ayungin boarding surfaces 250+ Sansha-registered vessels loitering 4–9 km away on the same day — a maritime-militia swarm corroborated from an entirely independent AIS source. Flagged in-app as correlation, not proof.

How it is built

  • Local — a zero-dependency Node server, bound to localhost only and explicitly never to be exposed without auth.
  • Hosted — a Cloudflare Worker with every route behind an HMAC-signed session; API tokens live as Worker secrets and never reach the browser.
  • Data — a versioned SQL schema (nine migrations) covering provenance, append-only custody, custody anchors, harm and venue extension, vessel minimisation, and population-aware harm views.
  • Interface — a naval-HUD map console: incidents as contacts, features as sectors, and a per-incident dossier carrying the legal mapping, evidence chain and corroboration.

The deployment is password-gated and the corpus is deliberately scoped, so there is no public link here. A walkthrough is available on request.

Project 03 — 2025 to present

the Margins Studio

An autonomous storefront agent, and the gate that stops it

A print-on-demand business that runs itself. Claude Vision analyses the source artwork; the agent selects Printify products, generates SEO-optimised listings, and publishes them to Etsy. There is no human in the loop — except at the gate.

The pipeline

  1. 1 · Vision. Claude Vision analyses the artwork — subject, palette, style, likely audience.
  2. 2 · Selection. The agent maps the analysis onto a product catalogue and picks what the design should be printed on.
  3. 3 · Generation. The agent writes the listing: title, description, tags, SEO surface.
  4. 4 · gate.js. Every generated listing is screened against quality, brand and IP criteria. Failures are blocked before publication.
  5. 5 · Publish. What survives the gate goes live automatically.
  6. 6 · feedback.js. A weekly digest lands in Telegram, measuring the agent's output against sales and listing quality — so performance is observed, not assumed.

Why the gate is the interesting part. Any competent developer can wire a model to an API and publish the output. The hard question — the trust and safety question — is what the system is not allowed to ship, who writes that rule, and what happens to the things it catches.

gate.js is a small, working instance of automated enforcement with a hand-defined rule set, an appeal path that runs through me, and a measurable false-positive cost. It is the same structure a platform runs at four orders of magnitude more volume.

What it demonstrates

  • Agentic pipeline design with a deliberate human checkpoint.
  • Automated enforcement against a written rule set.
  • A closed evaluation loop, measured weekly against sales.
  • Single-operator P&L ownership across four marketplaces.

Project 04 — prototype

SolBound

Incentive design and abuse resistance

A sober-to-earn recovery application built for the Solana Seeker device ecosystem. I designed the reward mechanics and the abuse-resistance model.

The central problem is a genuinely hard one: how do you reward a behaviour that only the user can verify, without building an obvious exploit — and without the exploit falling hardest on the people the system was meant to help? Recovery is exactly the domain where a naive incentive does damage, because the population is vulnerable and the failure is invisible.

Why it is on this page. It is the range project. Handa is guardrails on language; the Margins Studio is enforcement on output. SolBound is the third form of the same problem — designing a system that cannot be gamed by the person it is trying to help, and being honest about where that guarantee runs out.

Project 05 — 2026

The Canary in the Dragnet

Original policy research on AI surveillance and migration

The surveillance future we are warned is coming for everyone is already operational — on migrants, refugees and asylum seekers, who have the least legal standing to refuse it and the least visibility into it. Proposal abstract

The argument: border and refugee spaces function as low-oversight testing grounds for emerging technology. Biometric mass-collection, behavioural risk-scoring and location-data brokering are normalised on non-citizens first, then diffuse to citizens. Which makes migrant-rights failure a leading indicator of general AI governance failure.
